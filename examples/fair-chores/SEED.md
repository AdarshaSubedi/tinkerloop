# Project: fair chores in a shared home  —  seed   (EXAMPLE PROJECT — illustrative only)

- **topic:** How a household of 2–5 adults (roommates or family) can split recurring chores so
  that everyone actually feels it's fair — and keeps feeling that way after the first month.
- **the-assumption-to-attack:** *Fair means equal, and it's decided up front.* Chore charts, apps
  and house meetings mostly split work into equal shares (same number of tasks, or the same time)
  and fix the assignment in advance. That treats every task as costing everyone the same, and treats
  fairness as a property of the plan rather than of how the weeks actually go.
- **constraints:** No money and no special hardware. Evidence-surfaces: published research and
  surveys on household labor and perceived fairness; how existing chore apps and chore-chart
  methods work (their public docs / app-store descriptions); the user's own household (3 adults)
  as a 2-week paper or spreadsheet prototype. Any angle must be testable with a spreadsheet or a
  shared note, by people who are not technical.
- **novelty-bar:** Worth pinging the user only if an angle beats the equal-split chart on a named
  axis — fewer "that's not fair" arguments, fewer skipped chores, or less time spent organizing —
  or sidesteps a named bottleneck (e.g. one person always ends up "managing" everyone else).
  "Use an app with reminders" or "hold a weekly meeting" are recipes, not ideas.
- **kill-criteria (investigation-level):** Stop if the gathered evidence shows the problem is
  almost entirely about something no chore-splitting scheme can touch (e.g. people simply not
  doing what they agreed to, regardless of the split), or if every surviving angle needs software
  the household won't use.
